# Med Spa Owner Focus Group — Discussion Questions

## Warm-up

1. Tell us your name, your role, and a one-sentence description of your practice (services, size, how long you've been open).
2. On a typical day, how much of your time goes to *running the business* (software, admin, reporting) versus *practicing* or *managing staff*?
3. If you had to guess, how many hours a week do you or your staff spend on software-related admin — scheduling, charting, billing, reporting — combined?

## Current software landscape

4. Walk me through every piece of software or app you use to run the business — scheduling, EMR/charting, payments, marketing/texting, inventory, payroll, accounting, forms/consent, photo storage, review management.
5. Of those, which one is your "main" system — the one you'd say runs the practice?
6. Which of these tools talk to each other automatically, and which require you to manually re-enter or export/import data between them?
7. How did you end up with this particular combination — was it a deliberate choice, inherited from a previous owner, or added piece by piece as problems came up?
8. If you use more than 3 tools: has anyone ever tried to consolidate onto fewer systems? What happened?

## Pain points

9. Thinking about all the software you just listed — what's the single most frustrating part of your day-to-day with any of it?
10. Has any piece of software ever caused a real problem for the business — a lost booking, a billing error, a compliance scare, an angry client? Walk me through what happened.
11. When you want to know how the business is actually doing — revenue, provider performance, which services are working — how do you get that picture? Is it one report, or are you pulling numbers from multiple places and combining them yourself?
12. Do you feel like your software is better suited to clinical documentation (charting, consent, injectable tracking) or to the booking/client experience side — or does it genuinely do both well?
13. Does your monthly software bill match what you expected when you signed up? Have add-ons, per-user fees, or per-location charges ever surprised you?
14. Is there information about a client that exists in one of your tools but that you can't see from another — and does that ever cause a problem (duplicate outreach, missed allergy note, inconsistent history)?
15. For clinical documentation specifically — charting, consent forms, injectable/treatment mapping, photo before/afters — where does the software help you, and where does it get in the way or feel bolted-on?
16. Of these task categories — scheduling, charting/EMR, billing/payments, reporting/analytics, marketing/client communication, inventory, staff/payroll, compliance/consent — which are your top 2 most frustrating, and which top 2 "just work"?

## Day-in-the-life / task friction

17. Walk me through, step by step, what happens in your software from the moment a new client books their first appointment to the moment they leave after treatment. Where do things slow down or feel clunky?
18. Think of a specific task you or your staff do every single day that takes longer than it should. What is it, and why does it take as long as it does?
19. If you could wave a magic wand and make one task instantly effortless — what would it be, and what would "effortless" actually look like?
20. Is there a task you or your staff have just... stopped doing properly because the software makes it too annoying?
21. How do you currently train new staff on your software? How long does it take someone to become fully competent in it?

## Switching behavior & triggers

*For those who have switched software in the last 3 years:*

22. What finally pushed you to switch? Was it one breaking-point incident, or a slow accumulation?
23. Walk me through the migration itself — what transferred cleanly, what didn't, what took longest?
24. Looking back, was switching worth it? What would you tell a friend considering the same move?

*For those who haven't switched:*

25. Have you ever seriously considered switching and then decided not to? What stopped you?
26. What would have to be true for you to seriously evaluate a new system today — not just be annoyed, but actually start shopping?
27. What's the single biggest thing you're afraid of losing or breaking if you switched (client history, staff retraining time, consent/compliance records, integrations, reviews/reputation tied to a booking page)?
28. How long a transition period would you tolerate — running two systems in parallel, partial migration — before it felt like too much disruption?
29. Who else would need to be convinced besides you — staff, business partner, front desk?

*For everyone:*

30. If a new platform promised to solve the top pain points raised in this group, but required a migration, what would it need to guarantee for you to trust it with your data?
31. Have you ever been pitched by a new/competing software company? What made you say no?

## New software receptivity

32. Setting migration hassle aside for a moment — are you actively interested in changing software at all right now, or are you fairly settled with what you have?
33. On a scale of 1–10, how "stuck" do you feel with your current system — 1 being "I'd switch tomorrow if something better existed," 10 being "nothing could get me to switch"? Why that number?
34. What would a genuinely better version of your current software look like to you — in your own words, not a competitor's feature list?
35. If cost were not a factor at all, what's missing from what you have today?
36. With cost back in the picture — what would you realistically pay more for, if anything, and what wouldn't be worth paying more for?

## Non-software alternatives

37. If a new piece of software isn't the answer for you, is there something else that would make running the business easier — more staff, better training, a consultant, a bookkeeper, better internal processes?
38. Would you rather have better support/training on the software you already have, or a fundamentally different system?
39. Is there a manual workaround (spreadsheet, paper, sticky notes, a personal system) that you actually prefer to using software for a given task? Why does that one thing work better manually?
40. If someone offered to just fix the *connections* between your existing tools — so they talked to each other — without replacing any of them, would that solve most of your frustration, or is the frustration in the tools themselves?

## Wrap-up

41. Of everything we discussed today, what are the top 3 things that would most improve how you run this business — software or not?
42. What didn't we ask about that we should have?
43. Would you be open to a 20–30 minute follow-up call if we build something addressing what came up today?
