# Part 1: Competitive Analysis — Top 5 Med Spa Scheduling & Practice Management Platforms

**Platforms covered:** Zenoti, Vagaro, Boulevard, AestheticsPro, Mangomint

## 1. Executive Summary

There is no single dominant leader in med spa software — the market splits by practice size, clinical depth needed, and budget. The five platforms below represent the most frequently recommended options across 2026 industry sources (Capterra, G2, Software Advice, American Med Spa Association, and vendor-comparison sites).

| Platform | Best For | Starting Price | Clinical EMR Depth | Market Position |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Zenoti** | Large multi-location / enterprise chains | ~$225–500+/mo (custom quote) | Moderate | Enterprise scale leader |
| **Vagaro** | Budget-conscious, small–mid practices | $23.99–30/mo | Moderate (built-in EMR add-on) | Best value / broadest install base |
| **Boulevard** | Premium client experience brands | $158–421/mo per location | Light (no e-prescribe) | Premium UX leader |
| **AestheticsPro** | Clinical documentation & compliance | $160/mo (2 users) | Deep (medspa-specific) | Clinical/EMR leader |
| **Mangomint** | Modern independent/growing spas | $165–375/mo (new simplified pricing Aug 2026) | Light–moderate | Design & automation leader |

## 2. Platform Profiles

### Zenoti
**Positioning:** The enterprise-grade platform for large or franchise med spa operations.

- **Scale:** Used by over 30,000 spas, salons, medspas, and fitness studios in more than 50 countries, supporting tens of thousands of transactions daily.
- **Core strengths:** Centralizes scheduling, billing, marketing, inventory, payroll, and reporting into one database for a consistent experience across locations, with touchless tools like online booking, self-check-in, and digital forms. Handles digital consent forms and HIPAA-compliant patient notes, automates recurring "beauty bank" membership billing, and uses behavioral data to trigger win-back campaigns for at-risk clients.
- **AI/analytics:** Zenoti's demand-based pricing tools reportedly drive up to 6% higher ticket values, and high-adoption locations generate roughly $9,900 in incremental monthly revenue per location from booking, pricing, and personalization features.
- **Pricing:** No public pricing — all prices are custom quoted, with some med spas reporting $300–$600/month depending on features, and other estimates citing a starting point around $225/month scaling to $10,000–$15,000/month for large multi-location businesses. Add-ons (AI chatbots, reputation management, messaging) frequently cost extra.
- **Weaknesses:** Some users report building custom forms is time-consuming and that the implementation team charges for setup assistance; pricing opacity makes budgeting harder than flat-rate competitors.
- **Best for:** Multi-location or franchise med spas that need centralized corporate control and enterprise reporting.

### Vagaro
**Positioning:** The highest-value, most widely adopted platform across the broader beauty/wellness category.

- **Scale:** Supports over 90,000 businesses daily, with more than 136 million appointments booked and $4.08 billion in payments processed.
- **Core strengths:** Combines HIPAA-compliant EMR features — SOAP notes, digital intake/consent forms, photo documentation, and e-prescribe — with full practice management (booking, POS, CRM, marketing, payroll) in one platform, without needing a separate EMR subscription.
- **Pricing:** $23.99/month for a solo provider, plus $10/month per additional staff member (capped after 7 added employees), with no separate EMR fee — pricing is structured per bookable calendar and starts at $30/month/location, with custom quotes for multi-location accounts.
- **Payment processing:** Rates vary by merchant volume — 2.2% + $0.19 for large merchants, or 2.6% + $0.10 for small merchants.
- **Ratings:** G2 shows Vagaro at 4.6/5 across 368 reviews, with users highlighting easy scheduling and text reminders.
- **Weaknesses:** Clinical/EMR tools are less specialized than dedicated medspa EMR platforms; some reviewers note pricing can add up once add-ons are included.
- **Best for:** Small to mid-sized med spas wanting one affordable platform that covers booking, light clinical documentation, POS, and marketing.

### Boulevard
**Positioning:** The premium, client-experience-first platform for high-end med spas.

- **Core strengths:** Combines a clean, modern interface with tools designed to drive client engagement and retention, standing out in how it handles the full client journey from booking to checkout with features like two-way texting and automated marketing. Its proprietary "Precision Scheduling" feature recommends optimal appointment times to reduce schedule gaps.
- **Pricing:** $158/month per location (Essentials), $263/month (Premier), and $369/month (Prestige). Capterra lists a similar tier structure plus an Aesthetics Bundle for Medspas at $421/month.
- **Weaknesses:** EMR features are described as basic compared to dedicated clinical systems, with no e-prescribe capability, limited pricing transparency, and typical 12–24 month contracts. Client-facing intake forms and consent collection are less customizable than dedicated medical intake tools, and reporting is thinner than Vagaro's on multi-provider performance analytics.
- **Best for:** Premium, brand-focused med spas where the booking/checkout experience is a differentiator and deep clinical charting isn't the top priority.

### AestheticsPro
**Positioning:** The clinical documentation and compliance specialist, purpose-built for medspas.

- **Core strengths:** An all-in-one, HIPAA-compliant platform covering EMR, online booking, scheduling, CRM, marketing, point-of-sale, inventory, and staff management, with customizable treatment forms, digital consent, and before-and-after photo storage. Includes 500 ready-to-use intake forms and integrates with tools like Google Calendar, Zoom, Twilio, and Cherry.
- **Pricing:** Starts at $160/month for the Pro-Plus plan (2 users included), with additional users at $85/month up to 3 users; Executive is $285/month for unlimited users, and Enterprise is $350/month.
- **Scale/fit:** Well suited for small to mid-sized medical spas with up to 200 clinicians, with higher tiers scaling into enterprise use.
- **Weaknesses:** Lacks some of the more sophisticated clinical reporting features found in typical medical EHRs. Some users report burdensome costs for premium features and unexpected fees.
- **Best for:** Med spas where clinical charting, HIPAA documentation, and injectable-specific workflows are the top priority over booking-experience polish.

### Mangomint
**Positioning:** The modern, design-forward automation platform for growing independent spas.

- **Core strengths:** An intelligent, easy-to-use platform for growing salon, spa, and med spa teams with 2+ providers, built around automation to reduce admin work — including waitlist automation, customizable service rules, card-on-file/self-checkout, and membership billing. HIPAA-compliant, with integrated forms, charting, and CRM features designed for compliance, including retaining client data and documents for 7+ years.
- **Pricing:** Currently three flat-rate tiers — Essentials at $165/month, Standard at $245/month, and Unlimited at $375/month. Notably, a new simplified, more affordable single-plan pricing structure is set to launch August 1, 2026. Integrated Forms can be added to any plan for an extra $50/month, plus $25/month per additional location.
- **Weaknesses:** Does not include full EMR or clinical charting depth, making it better suited to spa services than clinical-heavy medspa treatments compared to AestheticsPro. Reviewers note the advertised plan price often understates real cost once features like two-way texting and payroll are added — budget 50–100% above the sticker price.
- **Best for:** Growing, design-conscious independent med spas (2+ providers) that prioritize a polished UI and automation over deep clinical/EMR functionality.

## 4. Positioning Map

## 5. Recommendation Guide

| If your priority is... | Choose |
|---|---|

## 6. Key Caveats

- Most vendors quote **starting** prices; per-user, per-location, and add-on fees (SMS marketing, forms, payroll, reputation management) can raise real costs 30–100% above the advertised tier.
- Contract length varies significantly — Boulevard commonly requires 12–24 month terms, while Vagaro and Mangomint offer month-to-month flexibility.
- Payment processing rates differ enough to matter at scale: on $25,000/month in card volume, a 0.5-point rate difference is roughly $125/month ($1,500/year) — worth factoring in alongside the subscription price.
- Pricing and packaging are moving targets: Mangomint is rolling out a new simplified pricing plan on August 1, 2026, so current published tiers may shift shortly after this analysis.

# Part 2: Is There Room for a New Med Spa Software Platform? A Market Opportunity Deep-Dive

## Bottom line up front

**Yes, there's room — but not as a generic "better booking app."** The underlying med spa industry is large and growing fast, the software layer serving it is still small and fragmented, and every incumbent has a structural weakness (pricing opacity, clinical-vs-experience tradeoffs, bolted-on reporting) that a focused challenger could exploit. The harder question isn't "is there room" — it's "room for what, exactly." Below is the case for and against, plus where the real openings sit.

## 1. The macro opportunity: a large, fast-growing underlying market

The med spa industry itself is expanding quickly, which matters because software demand tracks provider count and transaction volume:

- The global medical spa market was valued at $24.2 billion in 2025 and is projected to grow to $27.8 billion in 2026, reaching $78.2 billion by 2033 at a 15.9% CAGR.
- Mordor Intelligence projects the market reaching $26.2 billion in 2026, up from $23.29 billion in 2025, growing to $47.17 billion by 2031 (12.48% CAGR) — driven largely by rising demand for non-invasive and minimally invasive procedures like laser therapy, injectables, and body contouring.
- Technavio forecasts the market growing by $46.1 billion from 2026–2030 at a 23% CAGR, among the more bullish estimates.
- The US med spa industry alone is estimated at $21.4 billion in 2026.

Estimates vary by methodology (12–23% CAGR range), but the direction is consistent: **double-digit growth, U.S.-led, with new locations opening continuously.** More locations = more seats to sell into.

**Software market size, for comparison:** the medical spa software market itself is projected to exceed $2 billion by 2028. That's a small fraction of the underlying industry's revenue — typical for vertical SaaS, and a sign the category still has real headroom relative to the market it serves.

## 2. What's actually broken today (the openings)

Across reviews and industry research, four pain points recur constantly — these are your wedges:

### A. Reporting and analytics fragmentation
Reporting is the most frequently cited pain point among med spa owners, who often can't access unified KPIs across appointments, revenue, and provider performance without logging into multiple systems. Phorest's 2026 research confirms this is a top-of-mind frustration — when reporting lives separately from scheduling and client management, getting a full picture takes significant manual effort. No incumbent has fully solved this — even Zenoti, the analytics leader, requires heavy configuration to get there.

### B. The clinical-depth vs. experience-polish tradeoff nobody has closed
The market has a real structural split: platforms built for clinical rigor (AestheticsPro) versus platforms built for booking/brand experience (Boulevard). The med spa software market in 2026 has a clear structural divide between platforms built for clinical depth and platforms built for operational growth — very few succeed at both. This is the single clearest whitespace: a platform that is simultaneously EMR-grade *and* consumer-grade in UX doesn't fully exist yet.

### C. Pricing opacity and bolt-on fee creep
Every platform in the competitive analysis has some version of this: Zenoti's custom quotes, Mangomint's "budget 50–100% above sticker price" reviewer warnings, Boulevard's 12–24 month contracts, and per-user/per-location fees that make advertised pricing misleading. This is a trust problem an entrant can win on simply by being radically transparent.

### D. Migration pain creates real switching friction — but also real dissatisfaction
Med spa operators consistently describe past software transitions as painful — patient records stuck in PDFs instead of proper fields, lost historical data, medication lists that didn't transfer. This cuts both ways: it's a barrier to winning customers away from incumbents, but it also means unhappy customers are often *stuck* rather than satisfied — a large pool of switching-ready practices exists if you can solve migration credibly.

### E. Data silos across the broader tech stack
Beyond the core platform, med spas often run separate tools for booking, marketing, and client records that don't talk to each other — the "5–10 disconnected tools" problem.

## 3. Signs the opportunity is already being noticed

You would not be first to see this. Worth knowing before you build:

- **Decoda** (Y Combinator-backed) is explicitly building into this exact gap: an all-in-one EMR and practice management platform purpose-built for elective care, addressing the reality that med spas, wellness clinics, and aesthetics practices run on 5-10 disconnected tools that are expensive and error-prone. AI is woven into every workflow — an AI front desk that answers calls and books patients 24/7, an ambient scribe generating clinical notes in real time, and automated patient follow-ups.
- The broader healthcare AI funding environment is enormous and actively flowing into adjacent categories — healthcare AI funding hit $61.1B across 1,069 deals from 2023 through early 2026 — meaning capital is available for a well-positioned entrant, but also that competitors (well-funded ones) can move fast.
- Incumbents themselves are racing to add AI: Zenoti's AI Concierge/HyperConnect, Mangomint's Connect (AI-assisted texting/calls), and various "AI practice management" point tools (Freed, Canopy) are already stitching AI into adjacent workflows.

**Implication:** the "AI-native, unified EMR + booking + front desk" positioning is being actively contested right now, not wide open. If you enter here, you're racing Decoda and similar YC-stage entrants, not just the 2015-era incumbents.

## 4. Where the real whitespace is (ranked by attractiveness)

| Opportunity | Why it's open | Difficulty | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Unified, real-time cross-platform reporting/BI layer** (works *with* existing platforms, not against them) | Universally cited pain point; no incumbent has solved it well; could be sold as an overlay, reducing switching friction entirely | Low–Medium | Lowest-risk entry: you don't need to replace anyone's booking system, just aggregate their data. Fast path to revenue. |
| **Transparent, flat-rate pricing with genuinely all-inclusive features** | Every incumbent has fee-creep complaints; trust is a real differentiator | Low | Requires disciplined unit economics but no novel tech — a positioning and packaging play more than a product one. |
| **True clinical-grade EMR + premium UX in one product** | The structural divide nobody has closed | High | Highest ceiling, highest cost — building EMR-grade compliance (HIPAA, e-prescribe, audit trails) AND best-in-class consumer UX is a multi-year, well-capitalized build. |
| **AI-native front desk / documentation for elective care** | Real demand, but already being built by funded entrants (Decoda et al.) and incumbents bolting on AI | Medium–High | Competitive, not open — would need genuine differentiation (e.g., injectable-specific AI charting, better migration tooling) to win. |
| **Migration-first challenger** ("we'll move you from any platform, guaranteed, with zero data loss") | Named as a top fear/complaint; nobody markets around solving it directly | Medium | A go-to-market wedge more than a product category — pairs well with any of the above. |
| **Mid-market / franchise-tier alternative to Zenoti** | Zenoti's opacity and complexity draw real complaints at scale | High | Enterprise sales cycles are long and require deep feature parity (payroll, inventory, multi-entity reporting) before you can compete. |

## 5. The case against entering (be honest with yourself here)

- **This is a mature, well-served category**, not a greenfield. Five to seven credible incumbents already split the market by segment (enterprise, budget, premium UX, clinical, modern/design). A generic "we do it all" entrant with no sharp wedge will struggle to get initial traction.
- **HIPAA/EMR compliance is a real moat**, not just a checkbox — building and maintaining a defensible clinical records system (BAAs, encryption, audit trails, e-prescribe) is expensive and slow, and mistakes here carry real legal and reputational risk.
- **Switching costs work against you too.** The same migration pain that creates dissatisfied incumbents also makes practices reluctant to move, especially clinics with years of patient history in one system.
- **Well-funded AI-native competitors are already moving** into the most obviously "open" AI positioning (Decoda), and incumbents are adding AI features fast enough to blunt a pure "we have AI, they don't" pitch within 12–18 months.
- **Payments/processing economics matter more than people think.** Several incumbents effectively subsidize their software with processing fee revenue; a new entrant needs a real answer on payments (own processor, competitive rates, or partnership) or margins will be thin.

## 6. A workable path in, if you proceed

1. **Don't try to be all things on day one.** Pick one of the whitespace rows above — the reporting/BI overlay and transparent-pricing angles are the fastest, lowest-risk entries; full EMR+UX unification is the highest-ceiling but highest-cost.
2. **Design for migration from the start** as a core product feature, not an onboarding afterthought — this directly neutralizes the #1 reason practices stay put.
3. **Target the underserved middle**: independent and small-chain med spas (2–10 providers) are large in number, priced out of Zenoti's complexity, and often unhappy with Vagaro/Mangomint's clinical shallowness or Boulevard's cost — this segment is large, underserved on the clinical-depth-plus-UX axis, and less contested than enterprise.
4. **Be radically transparent on pricing** — genuinely all-inclusive flat tiers, no per-add-on fee creep, is both a real differentiator and cheap to deliver relative to building new tech.
5. **Validate before building deep EMR.** Talk to 20–30 practices in your target segment about what would actually make them switch — the pain points above are well-documented at the industry level, but your specific wedge needs direct validation before a multi-year clinical-grade build.

## 7. Sources & further research

Market-size estimates vary meaningfully by source (12–23% CAGR range) — treat any single figure as directional, not precise.

# Part 3: What "Client Management" Means in Med Spa Software

"Client management" (sometimes called CRM or client profiles) refers to the system that stores and organizes everything about an individual client beyond just their appointment slot. It typically includes:

- **Contact & demographic info** — name, phone, email, birthday, referral source
- **Visit history** — past appointments, services received, providers seen, frequency/recency
- **Preferences & notes** — preferred room temperature, allergies, product preferences, staff notes (e.g., "prefers quiet during treatment")
- **Clinical records** (when integrated with EMR) — SOAP notes, treatment consent forms, before/after photos, injectable mapping
- **Financial history** — packages purchased, membership status, outstanding balances, loyalty points
- **Communication log** — text/email history, marketing opt-ins, review requests sent
- **Retention signals** — churn risk flags, "miss you" trigger status, upcoming renewal dates

## Basic vs. deep client management

Basic client management (contact info + visit history + notes) is table stakes — every platform profiled in Part 1 has it. What varies is depth and integration:

- **Vagaro / Mangomint** treat it as a CRM layer bolted to booking — good for marketing and history, lighter on clinical depth
- **AestheticsPro** treats it as a true clinical record — SOAP notes, consent, injectable-specific charting live in the same client profile
- **Zenoti / Boulevard** lean toward behavioral/retention intelligence — using visit patterns to trigger win-back campaigns or personalize the booking experience

In short: "client management" spans a spectrum from **basic CRM** (contact + history) to **full clinical record system** (EMR-grade documentation), and where a platform sits on that spectrum is one of the clearest ways to differentiate the five platforms compared in Part 1.
