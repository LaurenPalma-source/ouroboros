# Pricing Strategy for a New Med Spa Software Platform

**Prepared:** August 2026

---

## The core recommendation: transparent flat-rate tiers + embedded payments as the real revenue engine

Two separate decisions matter here, and conflating them is where most incumbents get it wrong: **what you charge for the software subscription**, and **how you monetize transaction volume**. Get both right and you can genuinely out-position Vagaro, Boulevard, and AestheticsPro simultaneously.

---

## 1. Subscription pricing: simple, transparent, all-inclusive tiers

This directly attacks the #1 trust problem in the category (Zenoti's custom quotes, Mangomint's "budget 50–100% over sticker" complaints, Boulevard's fee-heavy add-ons).

**Principles:**
- **Publish real prices on your website.** No "request a demo for pricing." This alone differentiates you from Zenoti and puts pressure on Boulevard.
- **Flat monthly tiers by provider count, not by feature-gating core functionality.** Booking, EMR-lite charting, POS, and reporting should all be in every tier — the tiers should scale by seats/locations, not by which features you can afford. This is the opposite of the "$29/month becomes $99/month with four staff" trap reviewers call out.
- **No required add-ons for things practices consider table stakes** — digital forms, SMS reminders, and basic reporting shouldn't be $50/month bolt-ons. Save true add-on pricing for genuinely optional things (advanced BI, e-prescribe, multi-entity franchise reporting).
- **No long-term contracts.** Month-to-month, like Mangomint. Contract lock-in (Boulevard's 12–24 months) is a stated pain point — don't recreate it.

**Suggested tier logic**, positioned against the competitive landscape:

| Tier | Target | Rough price point | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solo/Starter | 1 provider | ~$40–60/mo | Undercuts AestheticsPro ($160), competitive with Vagaro |
| Growth | 2–10 providers | ~$150–250/mo flat | Sits in the underserved middle — more capable than Vagaro, more honest than Mangomint/Boulevard |
| Multi-location | 11+ / franchise | Custom, but with a published *floor* price, not total opacity | Compete with Zenoti on capability without the pricing black box |

## 2. Embedded payments: your real margin driver, not the subscription fee

This is the part worth taking seriously, because it changes what "the best pricing strategy" even means. Vertical SaaS companies that embed payment processing rather than just charging subscription fees see dramatically better economics. Andreessen Horowitz found that vertical SaaS platforms embedding financial services can grow revenue per customer by 2–5x, without adding new users or products — and this isn't theoretical: Mindbody, a direct comparable in this space, now generates more than 50% of its revenue from embedded payments rather than subscriptions.

Practically, this means:
- **Price the software subscription low-to-moderate and transparent** (your trust differentiator) — bundle SaaS fees and payment processing into one simplified structure rather than itemizing them, which both improves the customer experience and supports better margins.
- **Make your money on a modest markup on payment processing volume**, which is invisible to the customer as a "fee" in the way subscription add-ons feel — a platform capturing a favorable spread between its processing cost and what it charges typically leaves substantial money on the table if it doesn't own this layer, and med spas process meaningful volume (injectables, packages, memberships).
- This is exactly how Boulevard and Zenoti already make real money underneath their subscription pricing — you'd just be more transparent about it, and pass some of the advantage to customers via lower subscription fees, which becomes your pricing wedge.

---

## 3. Migration and switching incentives

Since migration fear is the #1 reason unhappy incumbent customers stay put, bake this into pricing rather than treating it as a support cost:
- Free, white-glove data migration (no "$X setup fee" — Zenoti and others charge for this)
- A time-limited discount or fee waiver for practices switching from a named competitor, positioned explicitly as "we know what it costs you to leave AestheticsPro/Boulevard — here's why it's worth it"

---

## 4. What to avoid

- **Per-feature gating on core functionality** — this is the single most complained-about pattern across every incumbent reviewed.
- **Usage-based pricing on appointments/bookings** — practices need predictable costs; this reintroduces the opacity problem you're trying to solve.
- **Undercutting on subscription price without a payments strategy** — pure race-to-the-bottom on subscription fees (à la early Fresha) is hard to sustain without the embedded-payments revenue layer beneath it.

---

## Bottom line

Price the visible subscription cheap and radically transparent — that's your marketing wedge against the whole category's trust problem — and make your actual margin on embedded payments, the way Mindbody and Boulevard already do quietly. You get to look like the "honest" option while running the same economics as the incumbents you're competing against.
